1. The idea — “More than most, and it shows”
Your logo has a wave curling over the word “Vision,” and your tagline is “more than most.” Your showroom is full of arches, curved counters, wavy louvers and an organic mirror. Nothing about your brand is a straight box — so the website shouldn’t be either.
The creative concept is called “Ripple & Doodle”. Two ideas run through every page:
Ripple — everything flows. Wavy section dividers, fluid blob shapes, liquid hover ripples, smooth scroll. The wave from your logo becomes the connective tissue of the whole site.
Doodle — hand-drawn marks. Little squiggles, circled words, arrows and stars that “draw” themselves on as you scroll. They add the friendly, human, “more than most” warmth and keep it from ever feeling corporate.
On top of that sit the three things you specifically asked for: a glass (frosted) header, a puzzle-piece testimonials section that snaps together, and a gallery that shows off several different animations. No boxy cards, no template feel — this is built to sit on Awwwards, not to look like every other laminate site.

3. The signature moments (the wow list)
Six set-pieces carry the “wow.” Spend the animation budget here; keep everything in between calm so these land.
1. Glass header
A floating, frosted-glass navigation pill that sits over the content with a soft blur, a 1px light border and a faint colour tint picked up from the section behind it. It shrinks slightly and its blur deepens once you scroll. Reference: Aceternity glass nav + Magic UI border-beam on the edge.
2. Puzzle-piece testimonials
The section you described: pastel jigsaw cards, each holding one customer quote, that snap into place one by one as you scroll until they interlock into a complete grid. Built with real jigsaw-edged SVG shapes (not rounded rectangles) in pink, blue, lime and peach pastels, animated in with GSAP (a spring “snap” on each piece, staggered). A last piece “clicks” in to complete the picture.
3. Wave / ripple dividers
Between sections, an animated SVG wave (straight from the logo) that gently morphs and drifts, and on scroll the next section rises up behind it. Uses GSAP MorphSVG-style path animation.
4. Doodle layer
Hand-drawn squiggles, arrows, circled words, underlines and little stars scattered with intent. They stroke-draw themselves on as they enter the viewport (GSAP DrawSVG-style). This is the single most “yours” element — no other laminate brand will have it.
5. Fluid blob shapes
Soft, slowly-morphing organic blobs in pastel tints sit behind headings and images instead of boxes — this is how we honour “no boxy designs.” They drift and squash gently (looping animation).
6. Liquid hero
The home hero: a WebGL (Three.js) fluid/ripple surface or an interactive gooey blob that reacts to the cursor, with the headline and a doodle underline on top. Optional lighter version: an animated SVG wave field. Always with a static fallback for low-power devices.
4. Colour palette — “Sunlit Studio”
Light and airy as your client asked — a warm cream base with soft pastels doing most of the work. The vivid pink, blue and lime (pulled from your logo and your showroom chairs) appear only as small accents: doodles, hovers, links, one word in a headline. Pastels dominate; the vivid trio pops maybe 5–10% of the time. That keeps it playful without becoming a “pop-colour” site.

11. Non-boxy design rules
Shapes: No hard rectangles for cards. Use arches, pills, blobs, or jigsaw shapes with soft or organic edges.
Dividers: Straight section edges are replaced with wave / curve SVG dividers.
Corners: Prefer generous rounding (24–40px) or true organic blob masks over 4px corners.
Images: Frame photos inside arches and blob masks, not squares (matches your showroom niches).
Depth: Avoid drop-shadowed boxes; use soft coloured glows and the glass/blur effect instead.
Layout: Let elements overlap and sit at slight angles — a scrapbook feel, not a rigid grid.

14. Performance & accessibility
Backdrop-blur (glass) is GPU-heavy — use it only on the header and a couple of cards, not everywhere; test on mid-range phones.
Lazy-mount the Three.js hero and WebGL gallery only when in view; static image fallback on mobile / low power.
Respect prefers-reduced-motion everywhere: doodles show already-drawn, puzzle pieces appear in place, marquees pause, blobs stop morphing.
Compress the 6720×4480 showroom photos to web sizes (WebP, ~1600px max); lazy-load; preload only the hero image.
Keep text contrast strong: ink on cream is fine; avoid pastel text on pastel.
Every interaction keyboard-reachable; visible focus ring (pink); alt text on all images; forms with labels + inline errors.
